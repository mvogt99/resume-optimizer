RISKS AND GAPS
--------------
RISK 1 — Microsoft certifications (MEDIUM, likely screening question)
   JD says "Azure Solutions Architect, Power Platform, or related" certifications are
   "highly preferred." Mike has AWS Cloud Practitioner only — no Azure certifications.
   Many Centric Microsoft practice roles use certifications as a screening filter.

   Recommended action (before or during application):
   Register for AZ-104 (Azure Administrator) or AZ-305 (Azure Solutions Architect Expert).
   Even having a scheduled exam date signals commitment. If not ready to commit: don't
   fabricate — be direct in interviews: "I've been delivering Azure data platform
   architecture at PwC and AHEAD without a certification credential, but I understand
   the importance in a Microsoft-focused practice and I'm committed to closing that gap."

RISK 2 — Dynamics 365 and Microsoft 365 depth (MEDIUM)
   The JD lists D365 and M365 as required Microsoft expertise areas. Mike's D365
   experience is as a data integration source system (ERP/CRM data models in CDM design),
   not as a D365 practitioner (CRM/ERP configuration, Power Apps extensions, D365 customization).
   M365 experience is similarly at the integration/governance layer (Azure AD, Entra ID,
   Teams as a data consumer) rather than M365 administration or collaboration platform work.
   If Centric's practice includes D365 CRM implementation or M365 workplace transformation
   work, this is a genuine gap.

   Suggested interview framing:
   "My Microsoft expertise is strongest on the Azure data platform and Power BI side —
   deep enterprise data architecture, governance, and AI. D365 I've engaged with as a source
   system in enterprise CDM designs and data integration architecture. M365 at the identity
   governance and data integration layer. If the Centric Microsoft practice extends into
   D365 CRM implementations or M365 workplace transformation, I'd bring in the right
   partner expertise while contributing on the data architecture and integration side."

RISK 3 — "Hunting" new business vs. practice building (LOW-MEDIUM)
   Centric wants someone who can identify whitespace, initiate executive conversations,
   and personally generate pipeline. Mike has done this as a practice builder (SPR from
   zero, NVISIA from zero), but his BD model was inbound/relationship + practice reputation
   rather than outbound enterprise account hunting. The resume frames it as equivalently
   as honestly possible.

   Suggested interview framing:
   "I've generated pipeline through the same mechanics — identifying companies that could
   benefit from Microsoft data platform modernization, initiating executive conversations
   through network and industry engagement, and converting those conversations into
   engagements. I've done it as a practice owner (which requires the same discipline as
   individual BD) rather than in a dedicated BD hunter role. The difference is I also
   delivered what I sold."

RISK 4 — Power Automate / Power Apps depth (LOW)
   Power Platform beyond Power BI is listed in the JD. Mike's Power Platform depth is
   Power BI. Power Automate is mentioned in the context of data governance workflow
   automation — honest but limited. Power Apps and Power Virtual Agents are not in
   the resume.

   Suggested interview answer: "My Power Platform depth is in Power BI and the data
   layer. Power Automate for governance and business process automation I understand
   at the architecture level. Power Apps I'd bring in a partner for the implementation
   while I architect the data model and governance layer behind the app."

RISK 5 — "Mid-to-large management consulting firm" experience (VERY LOW)
   JD says "experience within a mid-to-large management consulting firm preferred."
   PwC is one of the Big 4 — this is fully covered.
