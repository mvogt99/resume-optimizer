   The JD asks for "data mesh operating models" as a preferred qualification. Mike's PwC CDM
   work is domain-oriented and data-product-focused but was not branded or implemented as a
   formal data mesh program with domain ownership teams, self-serve data infrastructure, and

   aligned" without claiming a formal data mesh program.

   Suggested interview answer if probed:
   "My PwC Data as a Product framework was domain-aligned — separate data product ownership
   per business domain (orders, products, finance), conformed semantic layer, governed
   consumption. We didn't call it 'data mesh' but the principles were the same: domain
   ownership, product thinking, federated governance, self-serve consumption. I'm conversant
   in Zhamak Dehghani's framework and can apply the formal model to a banking data domain
   design."

RISK 3 — Feature store platforms (LOW-MEDIUM)
   The JD mentions "feature/embedding stores (where applicable)." Mike has feature-ready
   data product design and vector database integration but has not deployed a formal feature
   store platform (Feast, Tecton, Hopsworks, SageMaker Feature Store). Listed conceptually
   in competencies without claiming a specific platform.

   Suggested interview answer:
   "I've designed the data infrastructure that feeds feature stores — feature-ready datasets,
   versioned data products, pipeline governance for training/inference readiness. I haven't
   deployed Feast or Tecton as a standalone platform but understand the architecture requirements
   and the governance patterns for feature consistency across training and serving."

RISK 4 — Formal MLOps toolchain depth (LOW-MEDIUM)
   The JD asks for MLOps/LLMOps including model lifecycle, experiment tracking, evaluation,
   prompt/response logging, and deployment monitoring toolchains. Mike's MLOps experience is
   data infrastructure for ML (feature pipelines, training data, governed consumption layers)
   plus applied LLM/RAG patterns. He has not been the primary owner of an MLflow/Weights &
   Biases experiment tracking stack or a formal CI/CD pipeline for model deployment.

   Suggested interview answer:
   "My MLOps work is primarily on the data side — designing the data infrastructure that
   makes MLOps possible: feature-ready data products, versioned training datasets, inference
   data readiness, and governance for model/data lineage. For the ML platform toolchain
   itself (MLflow, W&B, model registries), I've governed the standards and design, partnering
   with ML engineering for the runtime implementation. In a banking context, the data
   architecture and governance layer is where I add most value."

RISK 5 — Bicep / CloudFormation IaC (VERY LOW)
