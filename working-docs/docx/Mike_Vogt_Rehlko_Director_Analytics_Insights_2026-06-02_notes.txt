RISKS AND GAPS
--------------
RISK 1 — Consulting vs. internal IT Director (MEDIUM, likely interview question)
   Mike's entire career is consulting / practice leadership. Rehlko wants someone who owns
   the analytics function internally — budget, headcount, multi-year roadmap, stakeholder
   relationships that persist year over year. Consulting engagements end; internal IT
   Director roles don't.

   Suggested interview framing:
   "My consulting roles have been structurally identical to internal IT Director ownership —
   I held the budget ($5M+ at PwC), owned the roadmap, led the team (50+), and was
   accountable to executive stakeholders for multi-year analytics delivery. The difference
   is I've done it across multiple organizations simultaneously, which has given me a wider
   view of what analytics maturity looks like at different stages and what actually moves
   the needle."

RISK 2 — Manufacturing as primary domain (LOW-MEDIUM)
   Manufacturing is preferred, not required. Mike has the AHEAD global manufacturing client
   engagement plus PwC water-distribution / supply-chain modeling against SAP HANA. Neither
   is a deep multi-year manufacturing IT career. Healthcare and financial services are
   stronger domains for Mike.

   Suggested interview framing:
   "My manufacturing exposure spans supply chain, commercial, and product analytics at a
   global labels and packaging manufacturer at AHEAD, and finance/supply chain domain
   modeling against SAP HANA at PwC. The data patterns — operational data, ERP integration,
   supply chain analytics — are consistent with what Rehlko needs. I'd pair that with a fast
   ramp on the specifics of power systems and generator manufacturing data."

RISK 3 — SAP depth (LOW-MEDIUM)
   Mike's SAP experience is SAP HANA as a data source and SAP BW/BW4HANA data structure
   alignment — not deep SAP Analytics Cloud, SAP Datasphere, or SAP BW administration.
   The JD says SAP is a must but doesn't specify which layer. If Rehlko uses SAP Analytics
   Cloud or Datasphere, this could be a gap.

   Suggested interview answer if probed:
   "My SAP experience is on the data architecture and integration side — extracting from
   SAP HANA, aligning canonical data models to SAP BW/BW4HANA data structures, and
   governing the analytics layer built on top of SAP source data. I haven't been the
   SAP administrator or SAP Analytics Cloud developer, but I understand the data model
   deeply enough to govern the platform and the team that operates it."

RISK 4 — Formal Agile CoE (LOW)
   The JD asks for someone who can stand up an "Agile/Delivery Enablement CoE." Mike's
   Agile experience is methodology adoption on engagements, not a named CoE with a charter,
   governance model, and formal training program. The resume frames this as "Agile delivery
   enablement" rather than "Agile CoE." If probed in interviews:

   "I've driven Agile adoption as a delivery culture embedded in every engagement — sprint
   cadences, retrospective-driven improvement, delivery standards. Standing up a formal Agile
   CoE with a charter and training program is a natural next step from that foundation, and
   one I'd approach the same way I've approached standing up three data and analytics
   practices from zero."

RISK 5 — Geospatial (VERY LOW)
   The JD mentions "geospatial capabilities" as part of the platform scope. Mike has no
   geospatial experience. Not included in the resume. If asked: "Geospatial is an area I'd
   bring in the right technical capability for — whether that's a team member or a vendor
   integration — while I govern the platform architecture and data governance standards
   around it."
