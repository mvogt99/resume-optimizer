This means the resume must lead with ORG leadership, DELIVERY discipline, PEOPLE development,
and OPERATIONAL excellence — not data architecture or hands-on technical depth. Technical
credibility is needed only to "engage credibly with principals and managers on architecture
trade-offs" — not to be the architect.

This is a harder internal pivot than Rehlko. Rehlko wanted an analytics executive who was
also technically grounded. Cedar explicitly wants an engineering leader who trusts their
principals to hold the technical vision and focuses their energy on making the org productive.

are supporting evidence, not the headline.

RISKS AND GAPS
--------------
RISK 1 — Epic / Cerner / Athena direct integration experience (HIGH, likely first question)
   Cedar's Integration Engineering team does bi-directional EHR integrations with Epic,
   Cerner, Athena, and others. Mike has HL7/FHIR compliance work (Advocate + SIH via
   Meditech, CMS Meaningful Use reporting at NVISIA; provider network + claims architecture
   at HCSC), but NOT direct Epic/Cerner/Athena integration engineering. This is the most
   significant domain gap for the integration side of this role.

   Suggested interview framing:
   "My EHR integration experience is on the HL7/FHIR standards layer — designing FHIR-
   compliant data platforms, CMS regulatory reporting from EHR source data, and multi-
   tenant healthcare integration architecture. I haven't been the team lead for an Epic
   or Cerner connection specifically, but I've lived in the same data model — ADT, clinical
   documents, claims, eligibility, provider — and know where the integration complexity
   lives. I'd lean on the Principal Engineers and experienced integration engineers for the
   Epic/Cerner specifics while I provide the delivery governance and team leadership that
   lets them do their best work."

RISK 2 — Product company vs. consulting organization leadership (HIGH)
   Mike's entire career is consulting / professional services / practice leadership. Cedar
   is a product company. The organizational dynamics are different: product roadmaps don't
   end after an engagement, engineers are employees not contractors, and the relationship
   between Data Engineering and Product Engineering is ongoing rather than project-bounded.

   Suggested interview framing:
   "Consulting practice leadership at scale is structurally similar to product engineering
   leadership — I've held the P&L, headcount, performance management, and multi-year
   delivery accountability that product engineering directors hold. The key difference is
   client context vs. internal product context. Cedar's three-workstream structure (platform
   evolution, client implementation, live operations) maps directly to how I've organized
   consulting delivery. I thrive in exactly the kind of fast-moving, imperfect-brief
   environment Cedar describes — that's what consulting has been every day for 20 years."

RISK 3 — On-call program and incident management ownership at product scale (MEDIUM)
   Cedar's Integration Support team runs a 24/7 on-call program for client SLAs. Mike has
   managed client-facing SLA commitments and incident response on consulting engagements
   but has not personally owned a product company on-call rotation or defined MTTI/MTTR
   targets for a production SaaS platform. This is a real gap but not a disqualifier — the
   Sr. Director role is to lead the EM who runs on-call, not to be on-call personally.

   Suggested interview framing:
   "I've owned client-facing SLA commitments and run incident response and postmortem
   cycles on regulated healthcare platforms. The mechanics of a product on-call program —
   rotation design, escalation paths, incident classification, postmortem culture — are
   things I understand and have applied in consulting contexts. I'd partner closely with
   the Integration Support EM and the Principal Engineer to define the right MTTI/MTTR
   targets and build the observability foundation that drives toward proactive reliability."

RISK 4 — Revenue cycle management (RCM) depth (MEDIUM)
   Cedar is a patient financial engagement / RCM company. Mike's healthcare background is
   payer/provider analytics (HCSC/BCBS claims and member enrollment, AIM Specialty Health
   provider network, AHEAD PBM). He has not worked in patient billing, patient financial
   experience platforms, or provider-side RCM (claims submission, denial management,
   collections). The claims data models are adjacent but not the same as RCM workflow.

   Suggested interview framing:
   "My healthcare data background is primarily on the payer side — claims adjudication,
   member eligibility, provider network — which gives me strong fluency in the data models
   Cedar's integrations touch. Patient financial engagement and RCM workflow specifics I'd
   ramp on quickly; the data architecture and integration patterns are closely related to
   what I've governed in payer/PBM contexts."

RISK 5 — Formal MTTI/MTTR metrics ownership (LOW-MEDIUM)
   Cedar specifically names MTTI and MTTR as operational metrics the Sr. Director will own.
   Mike has managed SLA performance and driven incident rate reduction, but has not been
   accountable to formal published MTTI/MTTR dashboards as a product company leader. If
   probed: "I've managed incident lifecycle from detection through resolution and postmortem
   on client-facing platforms. The formalization into published MTTI/MTTR targets and org-
   wide improvement programs is a natural step from that foundation."

RISK 6 — Managing managers (manager-of-managers) formal track record (LOW-MEDIUM)
   Cedar requires "4+ years managing managers." Mike's PwC director role (2020–2023) is
   the clearest evidence: director-level with a 50+ person organization, coaching leads
   evolving into management roles. The consulting context means these were not always formal
   "engineering manager" titles but the function was equivalent. If asked to be specific:
   "At PwC I directly developed and coached multiple team leads through their first formal
   management transitions — setting expectations, building their feedback and escalation
   skills, and giving them increasing autonomy to run their teams without escalation
   assistance. SPR and NVISIA were similar: practice leadership where I set the management
   standards for team leads accountable for delivery outcomes."
